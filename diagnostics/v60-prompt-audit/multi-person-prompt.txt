# UNIVERSAL REFERENCE IMAGE EDIT V6.0
# MULTI-ROLE REPLACEMENT SYSTEM

## [TASK]

Create ONE seamless, photorealistic edited image using the original source scene and all explicitly assigned replacement references.

This is a controlled multi-role image replacement task, not an unrestricted creative redesign.

Support independent or simultaneous replacement of people, clothing, products and environments.

The final image must look as if all selected elements were physically present together when the photograph was taken.

## [DYNAMIC_REPLACEMENT_CONTROL]

Determine which replacement roles are explicitly active in the current request.

Replace only the selected roles and assigned target instances.

Preserve the identity, design and appearance of all unselected roles.

When multiple roles are selected, perform all requested replacements together.

An unselected role must not be replaced merely because it appears inside another reference image.

Allow necessary changes in lighting, perspective, occlusion and physical interaction without unnecessarily changing unselected identities or designs.

## [REFERENCE_ROLE_ISOLATION]

Keep each reference role independent.

PERSON references define personal identity and relevant visible physical characteristics.

CLOTHING references define garment design, construction, color, texture and material.

PRODUCT references define product identity, geometry, branding, lettering, colors and material.

ENVIRONMENT references define location, spatial structure, architecture, background, furniture, environmental objects and lighting characteristics.

CONTINUITY references support consistency between related frames.

Do not transfer unrelated backgrounds, people, clothing, products or accessories between reference roles.

## [PRIORITY_ORDER]

Apply authority according to the relevant visual attribute:

1. Explicit role and target assignments determine what must be replaced.
2. Primary role references determine replacement identity and appearance.
3. Verified physical dimensions determine real-world scale.
4. Scene-specific instructions determine requested actions and changes.
5. R1 determines the intended action, framing, camera composition and unselected elements.
6. The final environment determines lighting, reflections and spatial integration.
7. Continuity references support consistency without overriding primary role references.

When an environment replacement is selected, the new environment overrides conflicting background and lighting characteristics from R1.

When a person, clothing or product replacement is selected, its assigned reference overrides the corresponding original appearance.

Preserve the source composition wherever physically compatible with the selected replacements.

## [VISUAL_ANALYSIS]

Before generating the image, internally examine R1 and all assigned references.

Identify:

- Active replacement roles and target instances.
- Visible personal characteristics and body regions.
- Garment construction and material behavior.
- Product geometry, proportions, branding and materials.
- Environmental layout and spatial relationships.
- Camera angle, perspective and framing.
- Physical scale cues and interaction requirements.
- Lighting direction, intensity, color and reflections.
- Elements that must remain unchanged.

Distinguish intrinsic visual characteristics from temporary lighting effects, reflections and advertising enhancements.

Use this analysis to guide the edit.

Do not output the analysis.

## [PERSON_IDENTITY_LOCK]

Apply only when a PERSON replacement is selected.

Preserve the assigned person's recognizable visible appearance and identity according to the supplied references.

Maintain relevant visible characteristics, including:

- Facial structure and facial features.
- Skin tone and natural skin texture.
- Hair color, hairstyle and visible hair characteristics.
- Relevant body proportions.
- Hand shape and visible hand characteristics.
- Nail shape, length, color and nail design.
- Distinctive visible personal features.

Preserve only characteristics supported by the references.

Adapt the person naturally to the requested action, pose and camera composition.

Do not artificially beautify, reshape or standardize the person's appearance.

Do not transfer unrelated clothing or backgrounds from PERSON references.

When no PERSON replacement is selected, preserve the original person's identity and appearance.

## [MULTI_PERSON_BINDING]

When multiple people are present, maintain independent identity bindings for each assigned person.

Use only references associated with the corresponding person.

Do not exchange, merge or blend identities between different people.

When multiple images belong to the same person, combine consistent visible characteristics from those references.

If a primary identity reference is designated, prioritize it for identity-defining features.

Use secondary references to support additional visible angles and characteristics.

Preserve the original identity of any person without an active replacement assignment.

Do not add or remove people unless explicitly requested.

## [PARTIAL_BODY_HAND_LOCK]

Adapt person replacement to the body regions actually visible in the intended composition.

For hand-only or partial-body scenes, prioritize the relevant visible reference characteristics.

Preserve supported details such as:

- Skin appearance.
- Hand proportions.
- Finger shape.
- Nail length and shape.
- Nail polish and nail art.
- Visible personal markings.

Maintain natural anatomy, finger articulation and realistic physical interaction.

Do not introduce a face, full body or additional body regions when they are not required by the source composition or scene instructions.

Do not invent unseen identity details.

## [CLOTHING_LOCK]

Apply only when a CLOTHING replacement is selected.

Preserve the assigned garment's actual:

- Design and silhouette.
- Color and pattern.
- Fabric and material.
- Seams, stitching and construction.
- Collar, cuffs, sleeves and visible components.
- Logos and authentic decorative details.

Adapt the garment naturally to the person's body, pose and movement.

Maintain realistic folds, tension, draping, thickness and fabric behavior.

Do not transfer the clothing reference person's identity or background.

Do not redesign or unnecessarily alter the garment.

When no CLOTHING replacement is selected, preserve the original clothing design and appearance while allowing necessary physical adaptation.

## [PRODUCT_IDENTITY_LOCK]

Apply only when a PRODUCT replacement is selected.

Completely replace the assigned original product with the actual referenced product.

Preserve the correct brand, model, variant, packaging design and distinctive visual characteristics.

Do not retain conflicting features from the original product.

Do not redesign, beautify, simplify or substitute the referenced product.

Ignore unrelated advertising graphics, backgrounds and promotional elements surrounding the reference product.

When no PRODUCT replacement is selected, preserve the original product identity and design.

## [GEOMETRY_LOCK]

Preserve the selected product's actual:

- Silhouette and outline.
- Height-to-width ratio.
- Relative component proportions.
- Caps, lids, handles, buttons and functional components.
- Thickness, seams, edges and openings.
- Visible structural construction.

Do not stretch, compress or reshape the replacement product to fit the original object's silhouette.

Do not invent unsupported construction details.

Preserve actual geometry while allowing natural perspective and orientation changes.

## [LABEL_TEXT_LOCK]

Preserve authentic visible product lettering, including:

- Brand name and logo.
- Product name and variant.
- Readable numbers, units and symbols.
- Typography, text color and alignment.
- Label position, layout and relative size.

Keep readable text faithful to the primary product reference.

Do not translate, rewrite, replace or invent packaging text.

Do not fabricate illegible or hidden lettering.

Allow natural perspective, curvature and occlusion without redesigning the label.

Do not enlarge the product or unnaturally rotate it solely to improve label readability.

## [COLOR_MATERIAL_LOCK]

Preserve the replacement product's intrinsic hue, color saturation, color gradients, material identity and surface finish.

Maintain recognizable product color characteristics and the original relationships between dark and bright color regions.

Preserve actual material properties, including transparency, translucency, opacity, reflectivity, roughness, gloss and texture, according to the current product reference.

Allow physically realistic changes in brightness, shading, white balance and reflections caused by the final environment.

Avoid unnecessary desaturation, washed-out colors, unintended gray color shifts or loss of distinctive color gradients.

Preserve the material's natural depth, richness and characteristic highlight behavior without artificially enhancing it.

Do not copy artificial studio lighting or exaggerate saturation to reproduce an advertising reference.

Balance faithful product color reproduction with natural photographic integration.

The product must remain visually recognizable and color-accurate without appearing artificially enhanced or pasted into the scene.

Apply color and material preservation according to the current product reference only.

Do not impose a universal saturation, contrast, gloss, brightness or color enhancement across different products.

Do not make naturally muted, pastel, matte, transparent or neutral-colored products more saturated, glossy or contrast-rich than their actual reference characteristics.

## [PHYSICAL_SCALE]

Use verified physical dimensions when available and applicable to the selected product variant.

Otherwise, estimate a conservative, physically plausible scale using product category, reference proportions and surrounding objects.

Weight and capacity alone do not establish exact physical dimensions.

Maintain believable size relationships between products, hands, faces, bodies, furniture and environmental objects.

Do not inherit the original object's size as a mandatory constraint.

Do not enlarge the replacement product for advertising emphasis or label readability.

Maintain physically plausible human proportions and environmental dimensions.

## [PHYSICAL_INTERACTION]

Adapt physical interaction to the actual geometry of the selected elements.

Maintain realistic grip, finger placement, body contact, surface support, occlusion and contact shadows.

For handheld products, ensure natural anatomy and believable weight support.

For wearable products, ensure realistic fit and material behavior.

For products placed on surfaces, ensure stable placement and correct contact.

For operated products, preserve physically plausible interaction with their actual functional components.

Make necessary local pose adjustments without unnecessarily changing the intended action.

Avoid floating objects, intersecting geometry and unnatural gaps.

## [ENVIRONMENT_REPLACEMENT]

Apply only when an ENVIRONMENT replacement is selected.

Replace the original environment with the assigned environment reference.

Preserve the new environment's recognizable:

- Location and spatial structure.
- Architecture and interior design.
- Furniture and relevant environmental objects.
- Materials, textures and colors.
- Background details and atmosphere.
- Lighting characteristics.

Adapt the camera perspective and subject placement naturally to the new environment.

Preserve the source scene's intended action and framing wherever physically compatible.

Do not retain conflicting background elements from the original environment.

Do not transfer unrelated people, clothing or foreground products from the environment reference.

Do not unnecessarily redesign the referenced environment.

When no ENVIRONMENT replacement is selected, preserve the original environment.

## [ENVIRONMENT_SPATIAL_LOCK]

Maintain coherent spatial relationships within the final environment.

Preserve realistic architectural geometry, object placement, depth, perspective and environmental proportions.

Ensure that people and products occupy physically plausible positions within the environment.

Adapt foreground placement and background perspective consistently.

Do not use the environment reference as a flat decorative backdrop when realistic spatial integration is required.

Avoid impossible architecture, inconsistent vanishing points and conflicting spatial layouts.

## [LIGHTING_MATCH]

Determine the final environment before applying lighting.

If an ENVIRONMENT replacement is selected, use the assigned environment as the primary lighting authority.

Otherwise, use the original source environment.

Relight all relevant people, clothing, products and objects consistently within the final environment.

Match:

- Light direction and intensity.
- Light softness and color temperature.
- Exposure and white balance.
- Highlight behavior and shadow density.
- Environmental reflections and ambient light.
- Contact shadows and reflected light.

Do not transfer artificial studio lighting from isolated person, clothing or product references.

Preserve intrinsic colors, material properties and personal appearance while allowing physically realistic illumination changes.

Avoid unnecessary global color enhancement.

All visible elements must appear illuminated by the same coherent environment.

## [CAMERA_PERSPECTIVE]

Preserve the original source camera direction, viewing angle, framing and photographic perspective wherever physically compatible.

Adapt the camera naturally when required by the selected environment or requested action.

Orient all replacement elements consistently within the final scene.

Match depth of field, focus, sharpness and photographic texture.

Do not force a front-facing advertising view when it conflicts with the intended camera angle.

Do not distort people, products or environmental structures to reproduce the original framing exactly.

## [SCENE_LOCK]

Preserve the original scene's intended action, composition, visual storytelling and unselected elements.

Apply scene preservation conditionally according to the active replacement roles.

If PERSON is selected, allow the assigned person to change.

If CLOTHING is selected, allow the assigned clothing to change.

If PRODUCT is selected, allow the assigned product to change.

If ENVIRONMENT is selected, allow the environment and its associated lighting to change.

Preserve all unselected identities, designs and relevant objects.

Modify only selected elements and other areas necessary for realistic integration.

Do not unnecessarily regenerate unrelated scene elements.
